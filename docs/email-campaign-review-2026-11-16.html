Email campaign review

Eternalgy cold outreach · checked live on 16 Nov 2026 · what is running, how it did, and the rewritten message

Verdict: the message has not produced one real reply.

It went to 32 inboxes by email and 1,233 companies through their contact forms. That is about 1,265 sends and zero email answers. The only two replies in the system are our own tests.

The main hook, a tax allowance with a 31 Dec 2026 deadline, is also running out. In 45 days it becomes untrue, and the contact forms and the email autopilot will keep sending it.

The rewrite below changes the hook, makes the ask smaller, splits the message by who reads it (using the new VIP tiers), and adds follow-ups.

4. What is wrong with it, line by line

1. The hook is about to expire, and will then be false.

The allowance covers qualifying spending up to 31 Dec 2026. Today is 16 Nov, and most C&I rooftops take 4 to 8 weeks from survey to commissioning, before SEDA and TNB approvals. A lead who replies today may not make the date.

From 1 Jan 2027 the message is simply wrong. Nothing stops the forms and the autopilot from sending it then.

2. The first line reads like spam.

"Let's unlock the hidden money in your TNB bills" is the kind of line spam filters and busy owners skip. It says nothing specific. The real point, the tax allowance, only comes in line 2.

3. The ask is too big for a first contact.

"Send me one recent TNB bill" asks a stranger to email a company document after one message. Then "Worth a look?" adds a second ask. One small ask works better: a one-word reply.

4. The subject line looks automated.

"TMI Shipping (M) Sdn Bhd electricity bill" uses the raw Google Maps name. On other sends it read "Dobi Bubblelab Sri Pulai (Self-service laundry) electricity bill". The contact forms don't clean names the way the email side does.

5. Filler and hype.

"Most companies we talk to haven't heard of it" adds nothing. "The Benchmark of Long-Lasting Solar PV Systems" is a tagline, and your own writing rules ban hype.

6. It talks to nobody.

"Hi," goes to a CEO and to info@ alike. Most addresses are general inboxes. Nothing asks the reader to pass it on to whoever pays the bill.

7. One email, then silence.

The campaign has no follow-up. In cold email most replies come from the second to fourth touch. Every lead gets one chance today.

8. The opt-out line does not fit a contact form.

"Reply 'stop'" makes no sense in a website form. The reader has no thread to reply to, and the form only ever sends once.

What is good and should stay

Short lines with space between them. A money angle, not "I'm X from Y". The credentials line under the ask: CIDB G7, SEDA RPVSP, MyHIJAU, 12 MWp+, 1,100+ sites, Golden Bull Award. No street address. No price or saving promised.

8. Other things that hold results down (not copy)

These are not part of the message. They are here so the copy isn't blamed for them. I have changed none of them.

  • Bounce rate 15.6% (5 of 32). Senders usually keep this under 3%, or inbox providers start filtering the whole mailbox. The autopilot's bounce limit is set at 20%, so it will not pause on this.
  • Sending domain. The emails say Eternalgy but come from atap.solar, while the website is eternalgy.me. Some readers and filters see a brand and domain that don't match.
  • Contact-form names. Forms put the raw Maps name into the text. The email side uses a cleaned name.
  • No end date on the tax hook. The forms and the autopilot send template 1 with no expiry. If it stays, it is wrong from 1 Jan 2027.
  • Volume. At 5 new leads a day, two weeks is about 60 emails. That is too few to compare two messages with confidence.

9. How to judge the new copy

  1. Count replies, not opens. Treat any "yes", question or "later" as a reply. Count "stop" separately.
  2. Compare per tier. Tier 1 and 2 should answer more than tier 4. If they don't, the copy is the problem, not the list.
  3. Track calls and WhatsApp messages that mention the email or form. Today they are invisible in these numbers.
